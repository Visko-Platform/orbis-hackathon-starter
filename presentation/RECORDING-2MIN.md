# Two-minute product recording

Record the actual app, with your microphone. Before starting, verify a live Orbis session and Nebius connection, close the credential dialog, and prepare the audience invitation in a separate browser profile. Use the local host and audience windows for this recording; the GitHub Pages website is a static demo. Keep the recorded sequence honest: show actual moving frames and the received scene; provider acknowledgement alone is not proof of visual compliance.

The intervals below total 120 seconds. Speak naturally and leave room for clicks and live response.

## 0:00–0:35 — Click through the cosmos

**Screen:** Start the cosmic opening. Click the projected targets through universe, cosmic web, Milky Way, solar system, Earth, San Francisco and theater. Keep the pointer visible.

**Say:**

“What if a movie audience could change the story while it was happening? This is Cutline. We begin with a journey from the cosmos to one theater in San Francisco. These destinations are selectable inside one continuous 3D scene. NASA imagery grounds the Earth view. And at the end of this journey, the audience becomes the director.”

## 0:35–0:50 — Reveal the live film

**Screen:** Enter the film and show moving frames with the choices visible.

**Say:**

“Here is Mara, alone on the last train. Visko Orbis generates the live film through Reactor. Now we give the room a decision: what should she do next?”

## 0:50–1:15 — Show a real audience vote

**Screen:** Open voting. Switch to the prepared audience window, cast a vote, then return to the host and show the updated tally. Close voting and direct the winner.

**Say:**

“I’m switching to the audience view and casting a vote. Back on the theater screen, the tally updates. When I close voting, Cutline freezes the result. Nebius turns the winning action into the next scene while retaining the character and story context. That direction goes straight to the running movie.”

## 1:15–1:35 — Show the response

**Screen:** Show the new beat and subsequent moving video. Let viewers see the actual result; do not hide provider delay behind an unrelated clip.

**Say:**

“Watch the next scene. This is the live response to our choice. Cutline distinguishes the instruction being accepted from new video arriving. The audience can see the story change, then choose where it goes next.”

## 1:35–1:50 — Show saved continuity

**Screen:** Open the timeline and continuity memory; point at the new saved beat and its choices. Do not initiate another generation during this short segment.

**Say:**

“Each decision becomes a saved story beat. We retain the character’s memory, earlier branches and the next choices, so the experience can continue beyond a single vote.”

## 1:50–2:00 — Close on the film

**Screen:** Return to the largest film view with the choices visible.

**Say:**

“Cutline brings live interaction into cinema. Visko creates the world, Reactor carries the live session, and Nebius directs its next beat. The audience changes everything.”

If the provider fails during the take, re-record after recovery or explicitly identify footage from the included earlier recording. Do not narrate a recorded fallback as a current live response.
