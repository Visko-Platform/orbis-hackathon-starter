We want AI to follow us around and help us.

But what if it did? Does AI actually know how to live?

One way: strap on a camera and show it your whole life.

That's imitation learning — the model copies what it watches you do.

But almost nobody wants to live on camera. The data never scales past a handful of volunteers.

What if we could simulate the world instead?

Train models inside the simulation — thousands of lives, lived at machine speed, no camera required — then bring what they learn back out to help us.

How does a generative simulator work?

The world is infinite — our simulator must be too.

So is the action space — anything you could do in a life, your Sim can try.

The real test: can models understand the world and plan on the long horizon?

Live world models like Orbis are perfect for this.

One loop: the world streams, the model lives in it, the harness keeps score.

Harness rule, in plain English: "Don't spend more money than you have." The simulator's goal can be many things — one of them is simply don't go bankrupt — and when the model pulls it off, the harness releases reward.

What we built this weekend

A shortcut that still learns.

1

Generate a synthetic symbolic dataset — thousands of episodes rolled out offline, no rendering needed.

2

Train a simple policy on it with Bellman updates — cheap, deterministic, runs on a laptop.

3

At inference, the policy drives — but a VLM infers the state from what it sees, turning it into a POMDP.

The model never sees the symbols at run time — only the world, the same way we do.

Every episode, every frame, generated.

We want super fast realtime video models for simulation. Orbis FTW!

We can watch our policy decide what to do!

Future work
